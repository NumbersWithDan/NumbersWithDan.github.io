# Dallas heat project code review and retraining

Updated October 17, 2026 after the complete source-data transfer. This supersedes the earlier incomplete-transfer and saved-model reviews.

## Outcome

The original idea is viable as a single-day, within-survey spatial analysis. A new pipeline rebuilds features from raw observations, Landsat bands/metadata/QA, EJScreen polygons, park/water polygons, and market polygons. It retrains models and makes the limits of the prediction task explicit. Every image currently used on the project page was regenerated. Actual geographic coordinates are omitted from public page assets; projected training locations remain in a private working directory.

At the selected 1 km working scale, nested spatial evaluation gives MAE 0.7628°F and pooled R² 0.2263 for the environmental XGBoost model. A predeclared 50:50 combination with distance-weighted nearby-reading interpolation gives MAE 0.7233°F and pooled R² 0.2957, versus mean-baseline MAE 0.8789°F. This is a 17.7% reduction in absolute error relative to that baseline, not a percentage-point increase in R². Large buffered regions remain difficult: fixed shallow XGBoost MAE 0.9270°F versus mean baseline 0.8888°F. The results support modest within-footprint interpolation, not reliable citywide or future-day prediction.

## Python review and changes

1. **Target and units.** The original response is recorded afternoon air temperature. The old standardized target was sometimes described as UHI intensity or surface temperature. The new response stays in Fahrenheit, avoiding whole-dataset target scaling and simplifying interpretation. Temperature, relative humidity, heat index, source IDs, and actual coordinates are excluded from environmental predictors. Coordinates are used internally to construct spatial tests and the explicitly labeled spatial-interpolation benchmark.
2. **Landsat preprocessing.** Original code hardcodes coefficients, omits explicit QA masks, permits invalid/near-zero index denominators, and writes NaNs while declaring a different nodata sentinel. The rebuild reads the actual metadata, applies the scene-center sun-angle correction to optical reflectance, converts thermal radiance to brightness temperature, checks grids, and rejects fill/cloud/dilated-cloud/cirrus/shadow/snow and radiometric saturation flags. Indices require valid nonnegative reflectance and a positive denominator. This is Level-1 top-of-atmosphere processing, not atmospheric correction to surface reflectance. The 183 masked point observations retain missing satellite predictors; targets are not dropped.
3. **Neighborhood features.** Added masked neighborhood means of NDVI and NDBI in 7×7 and 21×21 pixel square windows (210 m and 630 m wide), requiring at least 50% valid pixels. These are window widths, not radii or claimed cooling distances. No target values enter these features.
4. **EJScreen integration.** Original rasterization mixes width/height conventions, raster footprints were restricted to the survey area, and sample functions do not enforce valid coverage. The rebuild directly joins original source polygons, with exactly one row per observation and no off-extent zero filling. All 19,491 observations match an EJScreen block group. Negative indicator sentinels become missing, not real measurements. Traffic retains 290 missing values.
5. **Market-area join.** The old 10 m buffered join expanded 19,491 observations to 21,342 rows. The new join uses unbuffered polygons. Of the original observations, 497 have multiple containing/boundary matches; the smallest polygon area wins, with a stable polygon-index tie-break. A total of 12,222 observations have no market match and retain missing attributes plus an explicit missing category. This rule is deterministic, not a claim that the chosen polygon is uniquely correct. Landscape-only feature candidates test whether the market/context variables help.
6. **Geometry and distances.** All distances are computed in a projected meter CRS. One park geometry and three market geometries require explicit make-valid repair; repair counts are recorded. Lakes and streams are rebuilt from their original vector layers. The pipeline checks row cardinality, unique source locations, finite response values, raster transform/shape/CRS agreement, and NDVI/NDBI ranges. Intermediate cached tables and fitted models are saved privately, not over historical source files.
7. **Validation.** The original 0.01-meter groups are too small to represent neighborhood holdouts. New groups are explicit meter-sized cells, with two placements per tested size. All original observations are unique in the rebuilt table, so no duplicated temperature can cross a fold. Larger-region holdouts remove training points within 500 m of test observations. None of these spatial folds establishes temporal generalization.
8. **Tuning.** The original randomized search uses all observations before subsequent evaluation; its comparison cell imputes/scales before cross-validation. New model and feature selection occurs inside each outer training split: 3 inner spatial folds compare five configurations: landscape-only depths 3/5 and combined-feature depths 3/5/7, with regularization 15/10. The depth-7 candidate was added after an initial pass repeatedly selected depth 5; the repeated outer evaluation remains exploratory rather than a new independent test. There are 5 outer folds. XGBoost handles missing predictors directly. The full-data final fit is used only for exploratory explanations; reported prediction metrics come from held-out observations.
9. **Simple benchmarks.** A training-fold mean and a 32-neighbor inverse-distance-weighted interpolator accompany the environmental model. A fixed 50:50 blend was specified before the experiments and is evaluated without optimizing its weight on outer scores. It is a comparison candidate with local interpolation assumptions, not a citywide deployment model.
10. **Original prediction/scoring code.** The later finished notebook attaches real SHAP values, correcting the earlier zero-filled attempts. Other cells still mix old execution state, concatenate subsets with full frames, fit prediction-time encoders, or insert zeros for absent model features. Reproducing a saved fitted value does not verify that full procedure. The new page no longer relies on the historical citywide scoring map or its anonymous street example.
11. **Screening.** The rebuilt screening starts from observations at or above 106°F (the survey upper-quartile cutoff, including ties), not unvalidated citywide predictions. Among 5,214 hotter observations with all inputs, it averages percentile ranks for lower neighborhood vegetation, greater park distance, older housing share, and older-population share. Relative ranks and equal weights are explicit policy choices, not causal effects or a validated vulnerability scale. The anonymous example is selected for maximum screening score, not prediction accuracy; its held-out blended prediction misses the measured heat, which the page shows.
12. **Public artifacts.** Regenerated maps omit coordinate axes. The interactive map uses normalized schematic positions without a geographic origin or physical unit. Removed latitude/longitude fields from the prior example JSON and geographic bounds from the prior raster-check JSON. No original source datasets or trained location-bearing model are copied into the public website.

## Block-size investigation

Tested 100, 250, 500, 1,000, and 2,000 m, at shifts of 0 and half a block in both directions. Each combination has 5 held-out folds: 50 fixed-configuration model fits. Graph shading spans the two placements; it is not a confidence interval. The fixed environmental model uses 200 trees, depth 4, learning rate 0.045, minimum child weight 20, lambda 10, alpha 0.1, subsample 0.85, column sample 0.9, histogram bin count 128, and seed 42.

100 m blocks primarily test very local infill: median nearest training distance is 33–34 m, with spatial interpolation MAE about 0.43°F. At 1 km blocks, median nearest training distance is 289–311 m. A cell width does not impose an equal minimum separation across its boundaries.

The fixed-model 100 m out-of-fold residuals have pair correlation approximately 0.68 within the first 100 m bin, 0.26 at 100–200 m, 0.10 at 200–300 m, and 0.04 at 300–400 m. Three consecutive bins fall below |correlation| 0.1 starting at the 400 m upper edge. The predeclared working rule chooses the smallest tested block at least twice that distance: 1 km. This is a transparent heuristic informed by residual dependence, not a mathematically optimal block width or a range estimated from an independently confirmed variogram.

Pairs are sampled from 1,600 fixed-seed anchors, with up to 30 neighbors per 100 m bin within 2 km. Pairs overlap and are spatially dependent; the curve is diagnostic rather than an independence test. The same campaign informs the analysis design, so nested model tuning is conditional on the selected block design. A genuinely new day or additional routes would be needed for confirmatory assessment. The 2 km sensitivity and buffered-region results remain visible rather than selecting a favorable scale and hiding the rest.

## New image provenance

All nine analysis images on the page are generated by `render_retraining.py`: survey map, temperature distribution, masked-NDVI comparison, block-size sensitivity, residual dependence, nested-model comparison, held-out blended residual map, retrained environmental-model SHAP summary, and descriptive observed-heat screening map. No original exported image is used on the page.

SHAP uses the retrained final environmental model, exact tree-path contributions on a fixed sample of 1,024 unique observations (seed 42), with additivity checked. Contributions are in Fahrenheit. They explain that model, not the whole blend, and are not intervention effects. The map residuals are observed minus nested out-of-fold blended prediction, rather than errors from a full-data training fit.

Actual coordinates and fitted models are written only to a private `--work` directory outside the website. New public results are `retraining.json` and `page-evidence.json`; source hashes and coverage counts accompany the experiments. Earlier verification JSON and scripts are historical audit artifacts, not current retraining results.

## Remaining limits

The survey is one afternoon, while the Landsat scene was acquired roughly two months later. Top-of-atmosphere brightness temperature is not land-surface temperature; resampled thermal pixels do not provide native 30 m thermal detail. Route/time identifiers and another independent campaign would help separate geography from collection conditions. Block-group attributes describe areas rather than individuals. The block choice and screening weights remain analytical choices needing sensitivity and domain review. Stronger local fit is not evidence of a quantified tree-planting benefit or a neighborhood investment ranking.
