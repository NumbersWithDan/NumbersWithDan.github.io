Dallas Urban Heat Islands Analysis

A project on how heat, neighborhood design, and equity overlap across Dallas

Project Overview

This project looks at temperature patterns across Dallas neighborhoods to better understand urban heat islands and how unevenly that heat is distributed. I was interested not just in where the hottest areas were, but in how those patterns lined up with the built environment and neighborhood-level conditions.

Methodology

I combined satellite temperature data, census information, and geographic analysis to map differences across the city. The project included:

  • Satellite thermal imagery processing
  • Spatial analysis of neighborhood-level temperature patterns
  • Comparison against socioeconomic indicators
  • Statistical modeling of heat island intensity

Key Findings

The analysis found meaningful temperature gaps across Dallas, with some neighborhoods running up to 10 degrees Fahrenheit hotter than others nearby. Those differences were not random. They often lined up with the kinds of environmental and socioeconomic patterns that make heat more than just a weather problem.

Impact & Applications

I like this project because it points toward practical questions: where more tree cover might matter most, how urban design choices affect everyday life, and how data can support better planning decisions.
