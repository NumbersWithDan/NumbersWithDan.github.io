Reading List & Learning

A short list of books and resources I keep recommending or revisiting

On My Bookshelf

I read a mix of sci-fi, mathematics, statistics, analytics, and sports. Mostly, I pick up whatever looks interesting or fun.

Sci-Fi

I'm a sci-fi head. Alongside the math and data books, science fiction is a big part of what I enjoy reading.

Favorites

  • Dune by Frank Herbert
  • The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy by Douglas Adams
  • Parable of the Sower by Octavia E. Butler
  • The Murderbot Diaries by Martha Wells
  • The Three-Body Problem series by Liu Cixin
  • Anything by Andy Weir

Data Science & Analytics

  • "The Signal and the Noise" by Nate Silver - A reminder that forecasting is as much about humility as it is about models
  • "The Visual Display of Quantitative Information" by Edward Tufte - Still one of the clearest books on what good data communication looks like
  • "An Introduction to Statistical Learning" by James, Witten, Hastie & Tibshirani
  • "The Elements of Statistical Learning" by Hastie, Tibshirani & Friedman

Mathematics & Statistics

  • "Real Analysis" by Royden - The kind of book that forces precision whether you want it to or not
  • "Numerical Analysis" by Burden & Faires - A useful bridge between theory and actual computation
  • "All of Statistics" by Wasserman - Dense, practical, and worth keeping nearby
  • "Probability Theory" by Jaynes - A different angle on probability that makes you argue with the text in a productive way

Business & Strategy

  • "Thinking, Fast and Slow" by Daniel Kahneman - A useful check on how confident we can get in our own reasoning
  • "Moneyball" by Michael Lewis - Still a great example of how a shift in questions can change an entire field
  • "The Black Swan" by Nassim Nicholas Taleb - Uneven, but it does keep uncertainty front and center

What I Read

I mostly just read what sounds interesting or fun. Sometimes that's math or data, sometimes it's sci-fi, and sometimes it's something completely different.
