Statistics & Probability Theory

The math behind uncertainty, inference, and the way we learn from data

Probability Theory

This part of my coursework covers the theory that makes statistical reasoning possible: random variables, distributions, conditional probability, expectation, and the laws that govern long-run behavior.

Probability is one of those subjects that starts out intuitive and quickly becomes more subtle. That is part of why I keep good notes on it.

Statistical Inference

Inference is where sample data starts becoming evidence. I use this section for estimation, confidence intervals, hypothesis testing, and the assumptions that make those tools worth trusting.

Regression Analysis

Regression shows up all over my work, so I keep both the theory and the practical side close together here: model assumptions, interpretation, diagnostics, and the limits of what a regression can really tell you.

Experimental Design

Good analysis starts before the data is collected. These notes cover randomized experiments, blocking, factorial designs, and the small design choices that make later conclusions stronger or weaker.

Bayesian Statistics

I also keep material here on Bayesian methods, especially when prior information or hierarchical structure makes the Bayesian view a better fit than a standard frequentist approach.

Time Series Analysis

Time series work matters any time the order of observations carries information. This section covers forecasting, autocorrelation, seasonality, and the practical headaches that come with data measured over time.

Multivariate Analysis

When the number of variables grows, the questions change. Here I collect material on PCA, clustering, factor analysis, and other tools that help make sense of more complicated datasets.

This is where I keep the theory that supports a lot of the applied work elsewhere on the site.
