Real Analysis & Mathematical Foundations

The part of math that makes you slow down and justify every step

The Completeness of Real Numbers

Real analysis gave me a much clearer picture of what calculus is really built on. My notes here focus on completeness, limits, continuity, differentiation, and integration, along with the proofs that hold the subject together.

The completeness property of the real numbers sits underneath almost everything else. It is what lets sequences and series behave in reliable ways, and it explains why so much of calculus works the way it does.

Limits and Continuity

I use this section to work through the formal definitions of limits, continuity, and uniform continuity. These ideas are familiar from calculus, but analysis is where they finally become precise.

Differentiation and Integration

My notes on differentiation and integration focus on the assumptions behind the theorems we usually take for granted: when derivatives exist, when integrals behave nicely, and why the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus actually works.

Sequences and Series

Sequences and series are where convergence becomes more than a definition. This material covers tests for convergence, power series, Taylor series, and the habits of thinking that show up again in numerical methods and probability.

Metric Spaces and Topology

The later topics move into metric spaces and basic topology, where the language becomes more abstract but the big ideas from real analysis start to generalize in useful ways.

Applications to Numerical Methods

One reason I like analysis is that it helps explain why numerical methods behave the way they do. Concepts like continuity, convergence, and compactness stop feeling theoretical once you see how often they show up in computation.

About My Real Analysis Studies

Real analysis is where I learned to be more precise, more patient, and much less willing to skip steps that only feel obvious.
